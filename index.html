The work.

Six productions from a career in feature and episodic VFX.

Avatar

VFX Editor · 2009 · Framestore

Cameron's first film since Titanic. When I heard Framestore had it, I told my boss I wanted in. A year of sixteen-hour days, running the sessions where the supervisor reviewed every artist's work and presenting finished shots to production.

Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows

Lead VFX Editor · 2010–11 · Framestore

The final chapter of the saga, and a BAFTA winner for Part 2. I ran VFX editorial: gathering every asset, running the dailies, keeping editorial and the director in sync. The only person who knew the project structure from the earlier films.

Gravity

VFX Editor · 2013 · Framestore

An Academy Award winner, almost entirely visual effects. It took so long that Clooney's MySpace line had to be rewritten, because Facebook overtook it mid-production. Four years of shots, tracked frame by frame.

Alien: Covenant

VFX Editor · 2017 · Framestore

Ridley Scott's return to the franchise. Facehuggers and chestbursters are on screen for split seconds, enough to define a franchise's terror. I've been an Alien fan since childhood, and I made sure those cuts landed.

Black Mirror

VFX Editor · 2019 · Netflix / House of Tomorrow

Charlie Brooker's anthology on the cost of new technology. He demands effects you never notice, which is harder to pull off than spectacle.

The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power

VFX Editor · Amazon MGM Studios

Among the largest VFX undertakings in episodic television. Turnovers, tracking, versioning and conform at a scale where nothing can be done by hand.

What I actually do.

I own the editorial spine of a show — the system that moves thousands of shots from artists to screen. EDLs, turnovers, versioning, conform, under deadline pressure that never lets up. My job has always been the same: make the invisible work. After years of doing it by hand, I started building the tools to do it better.

  • AI integration for post

FRAME/SHIFT.

Every show I've worked on ran its VFX editorial out of spreadsheets — fragile, manual, rebuilt from scratch each time. FRAME/SHIFT is the alternative: a database built around how shows actually move, where the cut itself drives the pipeline. Shot tracking, turnovers and versioning derived from the EDL, not retyped into cells.

I'm building it the only way I'd trust: inside a live production, on real data, an hour a night. Not a startup. Infrastructure.
